\begin{abstract}
Visual counterfactuals should identify where a classifier can be
interventionally affected and then change that evidence without unnecessary
identity, locality, or semantic drift. Saliency alone provides correlational
localization, while permanently weighted diffusion guidance cannot reliably
express target-first priorities as denoising geometry changes. We introduce a
training-free framework that verifies semantic regions through paired
same-seed diffusion interventions, freezes their effects in a
classifier-specific influence graph, and resolves a minimal semantic support
for each source. Within that support, adaptive lexicographic trust-region
causal concept intervention (\method) measures the predicted-clean image,
requests attainable target progress, minimizes the best achievable
identity/locality violation, and only then minimizes non-target drift. On the
full paired end-to-end evaluation cohort, \method{} reaches
\EndToEndAdaptiveFRPct\% FR, COUT \EndToEndAdaptiveCOUT, and CD
\EndToEndAdaptiveCD, while retaining \EndToEndAdaptiveFVAPct\% FVA, FS
\EndToEndAdaptiveFS, FID \EndToEndAdaptiveFID, and symmetric FID
\EndToEndAdaptiveSymmetricFID. Relative to \bld{} under the same semantic mask
and attack protocol, it gains \EndToEndFRGainPctPoints{} percentage points in
FR and \EndToEndCOUTGain{} in COUT and reduces CD by
\EndToEndCDReductionPct\%. The causal claim is explicitly limited to the
frozen classifier--generator system rather than the physical world.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} visual counterfactuals, causal intervention,
semantic mask discovery, diffusion guidance, trust-region optimization

\section{Introduction}

A visual counterfactual explains a classifier by showing how an input could be
changed to obtain a desired output. For faces, crossing the target boundary is
not enough: the result should preserve the person's identity, avoid changing
unrelated predicted attributes, and restrict visible modifications to relevant
facial evidence. Otherwise, a plausible image may still be an unfaithful
explanation of the model.

Localized diffusion counterfactuals face two dependent problems. First,
localization must determine \emph{where} the generator is permitted to act.
Gradient saliency can identify pixels associated with a decision, but
association does not show that modifying a highlighted semantic component
changes that decision. Second, optimization must determine \emph{how} to edit
the chosen region. A weighted objective
\begin{equation}
 L=w_TL_T+w_DL_D+w_IL_I+w_LL_L
 \label{eq:weighted}
\end{equation}
must use one set of weights across changing denoising geometry and incompatible
states: reaching the target and preserving it after success. Strong
preservation may block the target, whereas strong target guidance can move
unrelated classifier outputs.

We connect these decisions in one training-free framework. Controlled masked
diffusion interventions verify which semantic regions influence the explained
classifier and form a frozen counterfactual influence graph. At inference,
the graph constrains source evidence to a minimal verified semantic support.
That support defines where change is allowed; lexicographic trust-region CCI
then determines how to change it. Target progress is primary, identity and
outside-mask locality form the best attainable safety envelope, and
non-target drift is reduced only inside that envelope.

The edited expression is localized to the mouth while facial identity and
background remain visually stable. These panels are directly exported from
the paired evaluation, not synthesized or retouched for presentation.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item classifier-causal semantic mask discovery through paired same-seed
        region interventions and confidence-based influence-graph verification;
  \item source-specific minimal region resolution using a frozen graph and
        source evidence, without inspecting or reranking generated candidates;
  \item predicted-clean lexicographic trust-region guidance and
        preservation-aware final restoration confined to the resolved support,
        improving FR by \EndToEndFRGainPctPoints{} points and COUT by
        \EndToEndCOUTGain{} in paired end-to-end evaluation.
\end{itemize}
Here, ``causal'' denotes an interventional effect inside a fixed explained
classifier and generator. It does not identify biological, anatomical, social,
or other real-world causes.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Diffusion counterfactual explanations.}
Generative counterfactual methods use structured latent spaces to produce
plausible alternatives~\cite{rodriguez2021dive,jacob2022steex}. DiME and ACE
combine classifier objectives with diffusion image priors
\cite{jeanneret2022dime,jeanneret2023ace}; TIME and ECED extend diffusion
counterfactual construction~\cite{jeanneret2024time,luu2025eced}; and DiG-IN
optimizes diffusion variables for model-analysis objectives
\cite{augustin2024digin}. A prior facial-counterfactual system combines
face-part masks, diffusion generation, and adversarial analysis
\cite{vohoang2026human}. Our focus is the connection between intervention-
verified localization and priority-constrained generation.

\paragraph{Saliency and mask-based localization.}
Grad-CAM and Grad-CAM++ localize classifier evidence through gradients
\cite{selvaraju2017gradcam,chattopadhyay2018gradcampp}, but their maps remain
observational explanations. Blended latent diffusion (\bld) confines synthesis
by restoring a correspondingly noised source latent outside a spatial mask
after each scheduler step~\cite{avrahami2022blended}. MaskDiME derives dynamic
classifier-gradient masks during denoising~\cite{guo2026maskdime}. In contrast,
we use Grad-CAM++ only to screen and resolve semantic candidates; causal
verification comes from paired diffusion interventions, and the selected
support is frozen before counterfactual generation.

\paragraph{Predicted-clean and multi-objective guidance.}
Latent diffusion samples in an autoencoder space~\cite{rombach2022latent}, and
DDIM supplies the scheduler used here~\cite{song2021ddim}. Universal Guidance
and diffusion posterior sampling demonstrate external measurements on
predicted-clean estimates~\cite{bansal2023universal,chung2023dps}. Gradient-
surgery methods modify conflicting multi-task directions
\cite{yu2020pcgrad}, but a weighted or projected direction still leaves the
priority policy implicit. We express that policy as sequential local
subproblems: attain target progress, minimize unavoidable safety violation,
then reduce collateral classifier drift.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:preliminaries}

Let $x_i\in[0,1]^{3\times H\times W}$ be a source image, $f_j(x)$ the logit of
attribute $j$, $T$ the explained target, and $y_T\in\{0,1\}$ its desired value.
The desired probability is
$p^\star(x)=\sigma((2y_T-1)f_T(x))$. Let $\mathcal R$ denote semantic component
regions and $R\subseteq\mathcal R$ an allowed region set.

For fixed generation seed $s$, define the paired intervention effect
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_{i,s,R}
 =p^\star\!\left(x_{i,s}^{\operatorname{do}(R)}\right)-p^\star(x_i).
 \label{eq:intervention-effect}
\end{equation}
The operation $\operatorname{do}(R)$ permits the generator to modify exactly
$R$ while source, prompt, seed, model, scheduler, and generation settings are
held fixed. This is a classifier--generator intervention, not a claim that the
semantic region causes an attribute in people or in the physical world.

\subsection{Overall Framework}
\label{sec:framework}

Figure~\ref{fig:framework} summarizes the complete method. A discovery cohort
is processed once to screen semantic candidates, estimate controlled
intervention effects, and freeze a reusable influence graph. For each new
source, the graph and source Grad-CAM++ evidence select one minimal verified
semantic region set. Selection occurs before synthesis and yields both a hard
semantic mask and a soft generation mask. The former defines the allowed
explanatory support; the latter provides smooth latent support for gradients
and \bld{} blending. Predicted-clean lexicographic guidance, DDIM sampling,
source blending, and final latent restoration then generate one audited
counterfactual without expanding the chosen region.

\subsection{Classifier-Causal Mask Discovery}
\label{sec:discovery}

\paragraph{Candidate screening.}
For each discovery source, Grad-CAM++ is intersected with semantic component
masks. Captured attribution mass and within-component density rank plausible
regions. This stage reduces the intervention search space but supplies no
causal verification: high attribution is treated only as a proposal.

\paragraph{Controlled region interventions.}
We generate singleton and selected joint region interventions under the paired
conditions of Eq.~\eqref{eq:intervention-effect}. The mask is the only allowed
source of spatial variation; prompt, seed, model, scheduler, and inference
settings remain identical. Post-generation attack is disabled so the measured
effect belongs to localized generation rather than a subsequent optimizer.
Alongside desired-probability change, the discovery record stores semantic
mask cost, strict outside change, identity, non-target drift, and joint-region
synergy.

\paragraph{Influence-graph verification.}
We aggregate repeated observations with a source-ID-clustered bootstrap, so
multiple seeds of the same source are not treated as independent images. A
target-to-region edge is verified only when the singleton region has positive
mean effect, the lower confidence bound exceeds zero, and the frozen minimum
sample count is met. Joint interventions estimate whether a union supplies
additional effect beyond its singleton members. The verified edges, costs,
confidence intervals, and synergies form a frozen classifier-specific
counterfactual influence graph.

\paragraph{Source-specific resolution.}
For a held-out source, we intersect its Grad-CAM++ map with its semantic masks
and consider only regions admitted by the frozen graph. Among verified unions,
we choose the smallest-cost set reaching the predeclared saliency-coverage
requirement. Resolution can use only the source image, frozen graph, source
Grad-CAM++ map, and semantic masks. It cannot inspect generated outputs,
rerank candidates, retry a failed explanation, or expand the region after
observing the counterfactual. This separation prevents output-dependent mask
selection from masquerading as discovery.

\subsection{Localized Trust-Region CCI}
\label{sec:trust-region}

\paragraph{Localized objective.}
For required desired probability $p_{\mathrm{req}}$, define
\begin{equation}
 \kappa=\log\frac{p_{\mathrm{req}}}{1-p_{\mathrm{req}}},\qquad
 c_T(z)=\kappa-(2y_T-1)f_T(x(z)).
 \label{eq:target-residual}
\end{equation}
The target requirement is met when $c_T(z)\leq0$. The resolved binary semantic
mask $m_s$ states where change is allowed to count, while a dilated, feathered
generation mask $m_g\in[0,1]$ supplies smooth latent support and source
blending. Non-target preservation includes all other classifier outputs:
\begin{equation}
 D_{\mathrm{NT}}(z)=\frac{1}{K-1}\sum_{j\neq T}
 \operatorname{Huber}_{\delta}
 \bigl(p_j(x(z))-p_j(x_i)\bigr).
 \label{eq:drift}
\end{equation}
Identity and locality define safety residuals
\begin{align}
 c_I(z)&=1-\cos(e(x(z)),e(x_i))-\varepsilon_I,\\
 c_L(z)&=\operatorname{mean}\bigl((1-m_g)|x(z)-x_i|\bigr)-\varepsilon_L.
 \label{eq:safety}
\end{align}
Identity uses a frozen FaceNet representation trained on VGGFace2
\cite{schroff2015facenet,cao2018vggface2}.

\paragraph{Predicted-clean measurements.}
At denoising step $t$, let $z_t$ be the current latent,
$\epsilon_\theta(z_t,t)$ the detached U-Net prediction, and $\bar\alpha_t$ the
scheduler cumulative alpha. For epsilon prediction,
\begin{align}
 \hat z_0&=\frac{z_t-\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,
                  \epsilon_\theta(z_t,t)}{\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}},
 \label{eq:pred-clean-latent}\\
 \hat x_0&=D(\hat z_0/s_{\mathrm{VAE}}),\qquad
 s_{\mathrm{VAE}}=0.18215.
 \label{eq:pred-clean-image}
\end{align}
The target, drift, identity, and locality models evaluate $\hat x_0$. Each
image-space gradient passes through the fixed VAE decoder,
\begin{equation}
 g=J_D(\hat z_0)^\top\nabla_x L,
 \label{eq:decoder-jacobian}
\end{equation}
but not through the U-Net. The decoder is therefore a differentiable
measurement map, not another diffusion transition.

\paragraph{Lexicographic trust-region update.}
Let $g_T=\nabla c_T$, $g_D=\nabla D_{\mathrm{NT}}$,
$g_I=\nabla c_I$, and $g_L=\nabla c_L$ with respect to $\hat z_0$. Generation
support is applied before norms, inner products, or optimization. If the target
is unmet, requested local progress is
\begin{equation}
 q_t=\min\!\left(\max(c_T,0),\;\chi\Delta_t\lVert g_T\rVert_2\right),
 \label{eq:requested-progress}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_t$ is the clean-latent trust radius and $0<\chi<1$. The first
subproblem finds the smallest unavoidable worst safety residual:
\begin{align}
 \tau^\star=\min_{d,\tau}\quad&\tau\\
 \text{s.t.}\quad
 &g_T^\top d\leq-q_t,\nonumber\\
 &c_I+g_I^\top d\leq\tau,\quad
  c_L+g_L^\top d\leq\tau,\nonumber\\
 &\lVert d\rVert_2\leq\Delta_t.
 \label{eq:envelope}
\end{align}
Inside that best envelope, the second subproblem minimizes collateral drift:
\begin{align}
 d^\star=\arg\min_d\quad
 &g_D^\top d+\frac{1}{2\eta_t}\lVert d\rVert_2^2\\
 \text{s.t.}\quad
 &g_T^\top d\leq-q_t,\nonumber\\
 &c_I+g_I^\top d\leq\tau^\star+\zeta,\nonumber\\
 &c_L+g_L^\top d\leq\tau^\star+\zeta,\nonumber\\
 &\lVert d\rVert_2\leq\Delta_t.
 \label{eq:drift-subproblem}
\end{align}
Once the target is met, its progress constraint becomes the guard
$c_T+g_T^\top d\leq0$. Deterministic bisection reduces an infeasible request;
an unreliable target gradient skips the update. Because only four masked
gradients participate, the subproblems are solved by active-set enumeration in
their span using a small float64 Gram matrix. The radius adapts from achieved
target progress: it shrinks below one-quarter of the accepted request and may
grow when more than three-quarters is realized at the boundary.

The accepted clean displacement maps exactly to epsilon-prediction space:
\begin{equation}
 \Delta\epsilon_t=-\sqrt{\frac{\bar\alpha_t}{1-\bar\alpha_t}}\,d^\star.
 \label{eq:epsilon-map}
\end{equation}
After the scheduler step, \bld{} restores source content outside $m_g$:
\begin{equation}
 z_{t-1}\leftarrow m_g\odot z_{t-1}
 +(1-m_g)\odot z^{\mathrm{src}}_{t-1}.
 \label{eq:bld}
\end{equation}
A permanent weighted composition applies the same nominal gradient mixture at
every iterate. In contrast, \method{} solves
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:envelope}--\eqref{eq:drift-subproblem} at the current iterate,
so its effective coefficients follow the current residuals and gradient
geometry.

\paragraph{Preservation-aware final restoration.}
The final post-\bld{} latent may remain below the target threshold because a
local step is attenuated by the scheduler or source blend. We therefore run at
most 12 restoration iterations on that actual latent. Each iteration
recomputes the two lexicographic subproblems through the fixed decoder and
evaluators and tests fractions $\{1,1/2,1/4,1/8\}$ of the proposed step. Before
target success, acceptance requires strictly improved desired probability
without worsening the best identity/locality envelope beyond tolerance. After
target and safety success, it must maintain both and strictly decrease
non-target drift. Every accepted step remains inside a cumulative clean-latent
radius; failure of every fraction stops restoration. This stage uses no new
U-Net prediction or scheduler transition. The saved uint8 image is evaluated
again because VAE decoding and quantization can move a boundary-adjacent result.

\section{Experimentation}

\subsection{Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\paragraph{Data and task.}
We evaluate $N=300$ eligible CelebAMask-HQ sources
\cite{liu2015celeba,lee2020maskgan} for smile removal
(\texttt{Smiling} $1\!\rightarrow\!0$). Source IDs are sampled once and paired
exactly across methods. The benchmark fixes the semantic mouth component for
every pair, isolating counterfactual optimization after semantic-support
resolution. Raw \bld{} and \method{} receive the same source, mask, prompt,
seed 42, diffusion checkpoint, scheduler, and localized smooth-boundary attack.
No output-dependent reranking or region expansion is permitted.

\paragraph{Generation and attack.}
We use Stable Diffusion 2.1 base with DDIM at $512^2$ resolution, 35 denoising
steps, classifier-free guidance 5.0, blend-start fraction 0.25, and a mouth
mask dilated by eight pixels. \method{} uses target threshold 0.8, clean-latent
radii $0.15/0.01/0.30$, target-progress fraction 0.9, reliability threshold
0.10, Huber transition 0.02, at most 12 final-restoration iterations, and
cumulative restoration radius 1.8. The post-generation attack is confined to
the localized support and follows a fixed perturbation schedule with boundary
margin 0.03. The same frozen 40-attribute CelebA classifier guides the attack
and CCI and is intentionally the source of truth for all classifier-dependent
metrics.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
FID measures source/output distribution distance using Inception features.
Symmetric FID (sFID) averages two deterministic cross-split Fr\'echet distances
to reduce split-direction dependence. FVA is the fraction of paired VGGFace2
embeddings with cosine similarity above 0.5, while FS is paired SimSiam cosine
similarity. FR is directional target success at the classifier's 0.5 decision
boundary. MNAC counts non-target binary attribute flips. CD sums absolute
correlations between each attribute change and the target-change vector. COUT
integrates desired-versus-source evidence while output pixels are inserted in
descending change magnitude. Thus FR, MNAC, CD, and COUT consistently explain
the same frozen multi-label classifier used by the method; they are not claims
of agreement with an independent human or physical-world oracle.

\subsection{Qualitative Results}

Figure~\ref{fig:teaser} complements the aggregate metrics with direct paired
comparisons. Raw \bld{} already confines the edit spatially, but its generated
mouth can retain stronger smile evidence. Adaptive CCI produces a more neutral
mouth while leaving face shape, hair, clothing, and background visually
stable. The full-face panels are intentionally shown rather than only mouth
crops: locality is meaningful only when the edited region is judged in the
context of the complete identity. Because every panel comes from the evaluated
cohort and shares the pair's source and settings, the visualization illustrates
the same comparison measured by Table~\ref{tab:end-to-end}.

\subsection{Discussion}

The result pattern reflects the lexicographic design. FR and COUT directly
measure progress toward the desired classifier state and show the largest
gains. CD also falls, suggesting that adaptive updates better decouple the
target transition from correlated attribute changes. By contrast, FID, FVA,
and FS are nearly unchanged because both methods inherit the same diffusion
prior and spatial blend. MNAC counts hard threshold crossings and is slightly
higher even as CD decreases; the two collateral metrics therefore capture
different behavior and should not be conflated.

The causal interpretation remains deliberately narrow. Paired interventions
identify effects under a frozen classifier, generator, prompt, and scheduler;
they do not establish that a facial component causes an expression in people.
Likewise, using one classifier for guidance and classifier-dependent evaluation
measures faithfulness to the explained model, not transfer to another model.
The local linearizations provide an auditable priority rule rather than a
global convergence guarantee, and repeated VAE/evaluator differentiation costs
more than raw \bld{}. Finally, face images and identity embeddings are biometric
data; the intended use is consented model auditing with appropriate data
protection.

\section{Conclusions}

We presented a unified framework in which intervention-verified localization
determines where a counterfactual may change and adaptive lexicographic
trust-region guidance determines how it changes. Paired same-seed
interventions turn saliency-screened semantic components into a reusable
classifier-specific influence graph; source evidence then resolves a minimal
semantic support without inspecting generated candidates. Within that support,
predicted-clean measurements and clean-coordinate trust regions make target,
safety, and collateral priorities explicit, including at the final post-blend
latent.

In paired end-to-end smile removal, the proposed method improves FR by
\EndToEndFRGainPctPoints{} percentage points and COUT by
